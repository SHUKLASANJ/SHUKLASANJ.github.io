01 — About

Fluids across thirty orders of magnitude.

I am a postdoctoral researcher in the Department of Applied Physics and Science Education at Eindhoven University of Technology, working with Prof. Federico Toschi.

My PhD at the Indian Institute of Science, Bangalore explored superfluid systems across an extraordinary range of scales — from quantum turbulence at the ~1 nm scale of a vortex core, to the superfluid interiors of neutron stars and self-gravitating condensates proposed as models of dark matter.

Today I work on multiphase flows, emulsions and odd viscosity in turbulence. Across all of it, my tools are statistical physics and large-scale direct numerical simulations on high-performance computers.

THEME 01

Quantum turbulence in superfluid helium

Quantised vortices make superfluid turbulence an intrinsically multiscale problem. Using the two-fluid Hall–Vinen–Bekharevich–Khalatnikov (HVBK) model and the Gross–Pitaevskii equation, I study its statistical properties, how inertial particles move in coflow and counterflow, and how nanomechanical devices capture and release single vortices.

  • Statistical properties of He II turbulence (Phys. Rev. E, 2023)
  • Inertial particles in superfluid turbulence (Phys. Fluids, 2023)
  • Capture & release of quantum vortices (Phys. Rev. B, 2025)

THEME 02

Neutron stars, pulsar glitches & magnetars

Neutron-star cores may host a neutron superfluid coexisting with a proton superconductor. I develop minimal Gross–Pitaevskii–Poisson models with a crust potential to explain pulsar glitches through vortex–flux-tube interactions, and to follow magnetic-field evolution and gravitational-wave emission in magnetars.

  • Minimal model for pulsar glitches (Phys. Rev. D, 2024) — featured in New Scientist
  • Superfluid–superconducting magnetars (arXiv, 2025)

THEME 03

Self-gravitating condensates & dark matter

Bose–Einstein condensation at cosmic scales offers a candidate description of dark-matter haloes. With large direct numerical simulations of the 3D Gross–Pitaevskii–Poisson equation I study gravity- and temperature-driven phase transitions, turbulence and the emergence of large-scale structures in bosonic and axionic condensates.

  • Phase transitions in collapsed axionic condensates (Phys. Rev. D, 2024)
  • Turbulence & large-scale structures (Phys. Rev. Fluids, 2025)
  • The Gross–Pitaevskii–Poisson framework (Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A, 2026)

THEME 04 · CURRENT

Multiphase flows, emulsions & odd viscosity

At TU/e I study classical turbulence in complex fluids: how droplets and ligaments deform and break up, how particles change that breakup, how emulsions form, and how odd (non-dissipative) viscosity alters the turbulent cascade.

  • Self-similar breakup of a liquid ligament with a solid particle (arXiv, 2026)
  • Odd viscosity in turbulence — ongoing
